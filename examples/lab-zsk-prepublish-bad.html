ZSK rollover (pre-publish), new ZSK used immediately

Zone bank.test. · conservative model · propagation 5m · registry delay 0s · max cache TTL 7d · start 2026-11-02 12:00 UTC
UNSAFESERVFAIL for up to 23h 12m

The new ZSK is published and used for signing in the same step, and the old ZSK is removed two hours later. Resolvers that still cache the old DNSKEY RRset cannot validate data signed by the new ZSK, and the mail record (TTL 1 day) keeps old signatures in caches long after the old ZSK is gone.

What breaks

T+0 (Mon 02 Nov 12:00 UTC) → T+1h 5m (Mon 02 Nov 13:05 UTC) · 1h 5m
From T+0 (Mon 02 Nov 12:00 UTC) the zone serves mail v1 (step "Publish ZSK2 and sign with it"), but resolvers may still hold DNSKEY v0 in their cache until T+1h 5m (Mon 02 Nov 13:05 UTC). mail becomes bogus: it is signed only by {ZSK2}, and none of these keys is in the cached DNSKEY RRset {KSK1, ZSK1}. Resolvers can keep answering SERVFAIL for up to 1m after that.
T+0 (Mon 02 Nov 12:00 UTC) → T+1h 5m (Mon 02 Nov 13:05 UTC) · 1h 5m
From T+0 (Mon 02 Nov 12:00 UTC) the zone serves www v1 (step "Publish ZSK2 and sign with it"), but resolvers may still hold DNSKEY v0 in their cache until T+1h 5m (Mon 02 Nov 13:05 UTC). www becomes bogus: it is signed only by {ZSK2}, and none of these keys is in the cached DNSKEY RRset {KSK1, ZSK1}. Resolvers can keep answering SERVFAIL for up to 1m after that.
T+2h (Mon 02 Nov 14:00 UTC) → T+1d 5m (Tue 03 Nov 12:05 UTC) · 22h 5m
Resolvers may still hold mail v0 in their cache until T+1d 5m (Tue 03 Nov 12:05 UTC), but from T+2h (Mon 02 Nov 14:00 UTC) they validate it against DNSKEY v2 (step "Remove ZSK1"). mail becomes bogus: it is signed only by {ZSK1}, and none of these keys is in the cached DNSKEY RRset {KSK1, ZSK2}. Resolvers can keep answering SERVFAIL for up to 1m after that.

Resolver profile: worst. A failed chain of trust is remembered for 1m and a failed answer for 1m. The zone itself cannot be validated for 23h 10m.

Corrected plan (YAML)
name: ZSK rollover (pre-publish), new ZSK used immediately
description: 'The new ZSK is published and used for signing in the same step, and
  the old ZSK is removed two hours later. Resolvers that still cache the old DNSKEY
  RRset cannot validate data signed by the new ZSK, and the mail record (TTL 1 day)
  keeps old signatures in caches long after the old ZSK is gone.

  '
zone: bank.test.
start: 2026-11-02 12:00:00+00:00
delays:
  propagation: 5m
margin: 1h
keys:
  KSK1:
    role: ksk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
  ZSK1:
    role: zsk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
  ZSK2:
    role: zsk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
initial:
  ds:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    ttl: 1d
  dnskey:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    - ZSK1
    signed_by:
    - KSK1
    ttl: 1h
  data:
    www:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK1
      ttl: 1h
    mail:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK1
      ttl: 1d
steps:
- name: Publish ZSK2 and sign with it
  at: 0s
  dnskey:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    - ZSK1
    - ZSK2
- name: Publish ZSK2 and sign with it (part 2)
  at: 2h5m
  data:
    www:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK2
    mail:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK2
- name: Remove ZSK1
  at: 1d3h10m
  dnskey:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    - ZSK2
Worst-case analysis: a version can stay in a resolver's cache for a full TTL after the last authoritative server stopped serving it. Conservative model = RFC 7583 assumptions (any cached versions may be combined, as for validators behind caches); fetch model = a validating resolver that validates on fetch and trusts its cache.
