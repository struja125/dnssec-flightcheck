TTL trap, lowering the DNSKEY TTL right before a ZSK switch

Zone bank.test. · conservative model · propagation 5m · registry delay 0s · max cache TTL 7d · start 2026-11-02 12:00 UTC
UNSAFESERVFAIL for up to 23h 6m

The DNSKEY TTL is 1 day. The operator lowers it to 5 minutes together with publishing the new ZSK, and switches signing one hour later, expecting the short TTL to apply. Copies of the old DNSKEY RRset were cached with the old 1-day TTL and stay in caches for up to a day.

What breaks

T+1h (Mon 02 Nov 13:00 UTC) → T+1d 5m (Tue 03 Nov 12:05 UTC) · 23h 5m
From T+1h (Mon 02 Nov 13:00 UTC) the zone serves www v1 (step "Sign with ZSK2"), but resolvers may still hold DNSKEY v0 in their cache until T+1d 5m (Tue 03 Nov 12:05 UTC). www becomes bogus: it is signed only by {ZSK2}, and none of these keys is in the cached DNSKEY RRset {KSK1, ZSK1}. Resolvers can keep answering SERVFAIL for up to 1m after that.

Resolver profile: worst. A failed chain of trust is remembered for 1m and a failed answer for 1m. The zone itself cannot be validated for 23h 5m.

      signed_by:
      - ZSK1

steps:
- name: Lower the DNSKEY TTL to 5m and publish ZSK2
  at: 0s

Worst-case analysis: a version can stay in a resolver's cache for a full TTL after the last authoritative server stopped serving it. Conservative model = RFC 7583 assumptions (any cached versions may be combined, as for validators behind caches); fetch model = a validating resolver that validates on fetch and trusts its cache.
