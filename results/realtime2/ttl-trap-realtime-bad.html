What breaks

T+20s → T+2m 2s · 1m 42s
From T+20s the zone serves www v1 (step "Sign with ZSK2"), but resolvers may still hold DNSKEY v0 in their cache until T+2m 2s. www becomes bogus: it is signed only by {ZSK2}, and none of these keys is in the cached DNSKEY RRset {KSK1, ZSK1}. Resolvers can keep answering SERVFAIL for up to 1m after that.

Resolver profile: worst. A failed chain of trust is remembered for 1m and a failed answer for 1m. The zone itself cannot be validated for 1m 42s.

name: ZSK used before the old DNSKEY RRset expired, in real time
description: 'Short TTLs so that the whole change runs in real time, with no compressed
  clock. The answers fail while the chain of trust stays valid, so this measures how
  long resolvers remember a failed answer.

  '
zone: bank.test.
delays:
  propagation: 2s
  registry: 5s
margin: 10s
keys:
  KSK1:
    role: ksk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
  KSK2:
    role: ksk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
  ZSK1:
    role: zsk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
  ZSK2:
    role: zsk
    algorithm: ECDSAP256SHA256
initial:
  ds:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    ttl: 2m
  dnskey:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    - ZSK1
    signed_by:
    - KSK1
    ttl: 2m
  data:
    www:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK1
      ttl: 10s
steps:
- name: Lower the DNSKEY TTL and publish ZSK2
  at: 0s
  dnskey:
    keys:
    - KSK1
    - ZSK1
    - ZSK2
    ttl: 5s
- name: Sign with ZSK2
  at: 2m12s
  data:
    www:
      signed_by:
      - ZSK2

Notes

  • info · plan: DS changes take effect 5s after submission (registry delay)
Worst-case analysis: a version can stay in a resolver's cache for a full TTL after the last authoritative server stopped serving it. Conservative model = RFC 7583 assumptions (any cached versions may be combined, as for validators behind caches); fetch model = a validating resolver that validates on fetch and trusts its cache.
